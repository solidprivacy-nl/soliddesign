# Workflow 02 — Design Direction

## Goal

Translate the diagnosis into a coherent, prospect-specific website direction before building.

## Decide explicitly

- core customer proposition above the fold;
- primary CTA and any justified secondary CTA;
- navigation and page hierarchy;
- hero structure and message density;
- trust/proof architecture using verified evidence only;
- service grouping and naming in customer language;
- visual personality appropriate to the actual business;
- typography, spacing, contrast and content density;
- image roles and art direction;
- mobile hierarchy and action placement.

## Image-role planning is mandatory

Do not design a prominent image container around whatever source image happens to exist.

For each prominent image role, define before selecting or generating the asset:

- purpose — what must this image communicate?
- subject — what verified or generic subject is appropriate?
- aspect ratio;
- focal point;
- intended crop/object-position;
- visual prominence;
- desktop behavior;
- mobile behavior;
- whether the image is evidence, atmosphere, service explanation or brand recognition.

Then choose the strongest truthful asset in this order:

1. strong real company/project image;
2. grounded art-directed visualization based on verified reality;
3. authentic sector-relevant photography;
4. high-quality generated image;
5. generic stock only as a last resort.

Weak source photography does not gain preservation rights merely because it is authentic. Preserve the verified subject matter or brand truth when useful; improve the presentation when the original pixels materially limit the design.

Generated or reconstructed imagery must not fabricate documentary proof. Do not imply an unverified staff member, premises, fleet, project, client or installation is real company evidence.

## Decision rules

1. Prefer one clear composition over a collection of unrelated sections.
2. Every major block must serve customer comprehension, trust, action or genuine brand recognition.
3. Do not add generic sections such as statistics, testimonials, logos or badges without verified content and a commercial reason.
4. Use customer language rather than database labels, audit terminology or internal jargon.
5. Do not force the same layout on businesses with materially different buying behavior.
6. Variation belongs in the design direction; do not create new SolidDesign infrastructure for one prospect.
7. Preserve identity; re-evaluate execution. Layout, typography, spacing and image treatment may change materially when that is what the diagnosed weakness requires.
8. Prefer bespoke, coherent art direction over collage-like reuse of mismatched source fragments.

## Output

Provide a compact design direction containing:

- design thesis in 1–2 sentences;
- proposed page/section order;
- hero proposition + CTA strategy;
- trust strategy;
- service/content strategy;
- visual direction;
- image-role plan for all prominent imagery;
- mobile-specific decisions;
- what will deliberately be omitted.

The direction should be specific enough to build from, but not a speculative design-system document.
